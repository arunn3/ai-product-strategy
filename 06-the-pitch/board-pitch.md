```markdown
**Thesis (1 sentence):**
Invest $200K over three months to make MMS the system that helps maintenance teams resolve operational issues faster and make safer scheduling decisions, beginning with a narrow workflow we can prove or stop.

**The case:**
1. Why now: We are already behind competitors on AI functions, and an entrenched vertical competitor could package comparable assistance into its existing maintenance suite within six months. This is not a proposal to “add AI”; it is a proposal to protect MMS’s relevance in the maintenance workflow by shipping a focused troubleshooting capability in four weeks, then earning the right to validate asset-level schedule optimization. Strategic-fit caveat: this advances the company thesis only if MMS is intended to own or deepen the maintenance operating workflow; if our strategy is merely to provide a general-purpose platform, this should not be funded.
2. What's defensible: The M2 moat is a customer-specific Domain Context Loop: approved maintenance content and operating context inform guidance; technician corrections and outcomes improve the runbook; validated learning makes subsequent guidance more useful in that customer’s workflow. Today that moat is weak—data-flywheel score 9/20, partial vendor portability, and no proven switching cost—so the interface itself is not defensible. The three-month objective is to prove that correction, outcome, and runbook data can become a differentiated asset before a vertical incumbent or OpenAI-based implementation reduces the product to a commodity chat feature.
3. The economics: $18 of estimated AI COGS against a $30 break-even threshold leaves no room for uncontrolled usage or untested outcome-based pricing. We will ship routing between lightweight and frontier models in H1 to contain cost, then establish an AI-adjusted gross margin, billing unit, and low/median/10× usage economics before any pricing commitment. The economics are not yet investment-ready; the funded work must show that the product can retain margin under heavier usage rather than merely demonstrate technical capability.

**The risks:**
1. Trust / failure modes: The unacceptable failure is the copilot directing a technician toward an unsafe or damaging action during a live production incident, or recommending a schedule change that causes an avoidable outage. The product will not auto-fix equipment; schedule changes require human approval; troubleshooting guidance must show uncertainty, retain an auditable record, and escalate after repeated failed attempts or a line-down condition. The current trust position is insufficient for scale: the 90% reliability target is undefined and the golden dataset has only six cases, so we must define failure categories, source requirements, confidence thresholds, and an expanded evaluation set before broader rollout.
2. Scale / governance: At 10× usage, cost, inconsistent source data, uneven answer quality across sites, and uncontrolled changes to runbooks will erode trust before infrastructure becomes the constraint. M5 provides the initial boundaries—no autonomous fixes, approvals for schedule changes, audit logs, weekly testing, real-time monitoring, and vendor-readiness testing—but not yet the required change control for model versions, retrieval content, learned corrections, or rollback. We will treat validated runbook updates as governed product changes, not automatic learning, and complete a user-side shadow-AI audit to identify unsanctioned workarounds and demand signals.
3. Competitive: We stop the scheduling bet if a vertical incumbent delivers comparable maintenance assistance embedded in the systems our customers already use and we cannot show a superior customer-context loop or measurable workflow outcome. Our immediate evidence gate is stricter and within our control: stop the Schedule Optimizer if, by week 8, it does not achieve at least 60% planner acceptance and measurable improvement in one agreed scheduling metric without operational disruption. We do not fund line-wide downtime optimization until the asset-level evidence exists.

**The ask:**
Approve $200K, three dedicated people, and three months for a bounded validation program: a product lead, an applied AI/data engineer, and a maintenance-domain lead, with shared design-partner, security, and platform support. The investment delivers an H1 troubleshooting release with auditability and escalation, cost-routing controls, an asset-level scheduling pilot, a defined reliability contract and expanded evaluation dataset, and a customer-site unit-economics decision package. In exchange, we pause the line-wide Downtime Reducer and Work Instruction Generator; neither receives build funding until troubleshooting adoption, schedule acceptance, trust controls, and margin evidence support a go decision.
```
