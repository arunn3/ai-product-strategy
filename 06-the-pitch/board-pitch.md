**Thesis (1 sentence):**
MMS-AI will improve maintenance response and scheduling decisions for plant teams by turning existing maintenance knowledge into guided, auditable support—starting with troubleshooting, not broad automation.

**The case:**
1. Why now: The immediate timing window is competitive, not technological: an entrenched vertical competitor could offer a comparable assistant within six months. We should use the next 4–12 weeks to prove a narrow maintenance workflow before this becomes a bundled feature elsewhere; the strategy does not yet contain external market evidence, customer demand evidence, or a quantified timing signal beyond that competitive risk.
2. What's defensible: The intended moat is the M2 Domain Context Loop: user corrections and outcomes improve customer-specific runbooks, which should make future guidance more relevant than a generic assistant. Today this is a weak moat, scored 9/20 on the data flywheel, with no demonstrated switching cost or cross-domain advantage; it will not be defensible in six months unless we capture validated maintenance context, outcomes, and workflow integrations faster than competitors can replicate the interface.
3. The economics: AI COGS are currently estimated at $18 per unit against a $30 break-even threshold, with a stated current gross margin of $5. The routing plan—lightweight-model triage with frontier-model escalation—is the right first cost control, but pricing, AI-adjusted gross margin, net-margin shift, billing unit, and power-user economics are incomplete; do not commit to outcome-based pricing until the customer value metric and stress-tested margin model are defined.

**The risks:**
1. Trust / failure modes: The front-page failure is guidance that causes a technician to take an unsafe or damaging action during a production-line issue, or a schedule recommendation that creates an avoidable outage. The intended controls are uncertainty display, cited and auditable guidance, escalation after repeated failed attempts or line-down conditions, and human approval before applying schedule changes; however, the reliability promise is not credible yet because “90% reliability” is undefined and the golden dataset contains only six cases.
2. Scale / governance: At 10x usage, the first failure points will be inference cost, inconsistent answer quality across assets and sites, incomplete source data, and trust erosion from ungoverned learned content. M5 establishes useful foundations—no autonomous fixing, approval for scheduling changes, audit logs, weekly tests, real-time monitoring, and monthly vendor-portability checks—but it lacks change control for models and runbooks, a defined approval flow for corrections, and a completed shadow-AI audit.
3. Competitive: The competitive kill scenario is a vertical maintenance or industrial-software incumbent shipping comparable troubleshooting and scheduling support, embedded in the systems our users already operate, before we establish a differentiated customer-context loop. We have no explicit competitive kill criterion today; the near-term product kill criterion is more concrete: stop the Schedule Optimizer if, by week 8, we do not achieve at least 60% planner acceptance and a measurable improvement in an agreed scheduling metric.

**The ask:**
Gap: the strategy does not yet specify a credible dollar amount, headcount, accountable owners, or a trade-off, so leadership cannot make an investment decision as written. Request a 12-week, tightly bounded validation allocation for one product lead, one maintenance-domain lead, one applied-AI/data engineer, and shared design-partner and platform support; fund only the H1 troubleshooting release, routing economics, and one asset-level scheduling pilot. In return, leadership gets a decision package at week 8–12: adoption and acceptance data, a defined reliability contract and expanded evaluation set, validated unit economics, and a go/stop recommendation. If funded, pause the line-wide Downtime Reducer and Work Instruction Generator until the core troubleshooting and asset-level scheduling evidence is proven.
