Why now

The immediate timing window is competitive, not technological: an entrenched vertical competitor could offer a comparable assistant within six months.
We should use the next 4–12 weeks to prove a narrow maintenance workflow before this becomes a bundled feature elsewhere; the strategy does not yet contain external market evidence, customer demand evidence, or a quantified timing signal beyond that competitive risk.

The Risks

What could break this

Trust / Failure
The front-page failure is guidance that causes a technician to take an unsafe or damaging action during a production-line issue, or a schedule recommendation that creates an avoidable outage. The intended controls are uncertainty display, cited and auditable guidance, escalation after repeated failed attempts or line-down conditions, and human approval before applying schedule changes; however, the reliability promise is not credible yet because “90% reliability” is undefined and the golden dataset contains only six cases.
Scale / Governance
At 10x usage, the first failure points will be inference cost, inconsistent answer quality across assets and sites, incomplete source data, and trust erosion from ungoverned learned content. M5 establishes useful foundations—no autonomous fixing, approval for scheduling changes, audit logs, weekly tests, real-time monitoring, and monthly vendor-portability checks—but it lacks change control for models and runbooks, a defined approval flow for corrections, and a completed shadow-AI audit.
Competitive
The competitive kill scenario is a vertical maintenance or industrial-software incumbent shipping comparable troubleshooting and scheduling support, embedded in the systems our users already operate, before we establish a differentiated customer-context loop. We have no explicit competitive kill criterion today; the near-term product kill criterion is more concrete: stop the Schedule Optimizer if, by week 8, we do not achieve at least 60% planner acceptance and a measurable improvement in an agreed scheduling metric.

The Ask

What we're asking for

Gap: the strategy does not yet specify a credible dollar amount, headcount, accountable owners, or a trade-off, so leadership cannot make an investment decision as written. Request a 12-week, tightly bounded validation allocation for one product lead, one maintenance-domain lead, one applied-AI/data engineer, and shared design-partner and platform support; fund only the H1 troubleshooting release, routing economics, and one asset-level scheduling pilot. In return, leadership gets a decision package at week 8–12: adoption and acceptance data, a defined reliability contract and expanded evaluation set, validated unit economics, and a go/stop recommendation. If funded, pause the line-wide Downtime Reducer and Work Instruction Generator until the core troubleshooting and asset-level scheduling evidence is proven.
